The day, on the screen

Four lights. One clock.

01

Morning

Cool, bright light as soon as you wake. Melanopsin wants a strong day signal here. Ember raises it in about 25 minutes.

02

Day

Full color at about 6500K. The screen stays out of the way while you work.

03

Evening

Starts at sunset or three hours before bed, whichever comes first. Blue light drops fast in the first 40 minutes.

04

Night

Warm and dim until morning. Pause one hour when you need true color.

How it works

Install it. Forget it.

  1. 01

    Open Ember

    It lives in the menu bar and starts at login. No dock icon. No account.

  2. 02

    Set wake and bed

    Two times. Location fills sunrise and sunset. Brunswick, Georgia is the fallback.

  3. 03

    Leave it on

    Ember turns Night Shift off and quits f.lux so they do not fight. Photos, Figma, and Photoshop get true color automatically.

Not f.lux

Sunset orange is not a day.

f.lux

Waits for sunset, then tints the screen orange. Day is unmodified. Night is a filter.

Ember

Follows wake, bed, and the sun. Cuts the blue primary that hits melanopsin. Dims at night. Cool light in the morning, not only warmth at dusk.

Questions

Short answers

Will this fix my sleep?
It helps the screen stop fighting your clock. It cannot replace daylight, a dark room, or a doctor. The panel says so.
Does it collect data?
No account. No telemetry. Location stays on the Mac, for sun times only.
What about Night Shift?
Ember turns it off while Ember is on. Two systems fighting makes the color worse.
Can I pause it?
Yes. One hour of true color, then it returns. Use that for photos or video.
Does a screen count as daylight?
No. Indoor day targets are about 250 melanopic lux at the eye. A display cannot hit that. Ember only fixes the display’s share.
Mac only?
Yes. macOS 14 or newer. Apple silicon.
